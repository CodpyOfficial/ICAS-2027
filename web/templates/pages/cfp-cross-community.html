Autonomous systems sit at the crossroads of many fields. Cross-community special sessions bring researchers from neighbouring communities to {{.Site.Conference.FullTitle}} around a shared problem.

What makes a session cross-community

  • The session is organized jointly by members of at least two different research communities — for example robotics, control, machine learning, transportation, aerospace, human–computer interaction, safety engineering or law and policy.
  • The topic genuinely needs both perspectives, and the speakers reflect that mix.
  • Organizers ideally have experience running sessions or workshops in each of the communities involved.

Example themes

{{icon "car"}} Autonomy & transportation

Connected and automated mobility, traffic management and infrastructure.

{{icon "shield-check"}} Autonomy & safety engineering

Assurance cases, hazard analysis and certification of learning-enabled components.

{{icon "user-check"}} Autonomy & human factors

Trust calibration, explainability and shared control.

{{icon "scale"}} Autonomy & policy

Accountability, standards, regulation and labour impact.

Proposal contents

  1. Title and abstract of the session.
  2. Rationale — why the topic needs more than one community, and why now.
  3. Proposed speakers and talks, showing the mix of communities.
  4. Short biographies of the organizers and the communities they represent.
  5. A note of support from each partner community or event, if available.

Proposals are evaluated with the same criteria as special sessions; dates follow the special session call.
